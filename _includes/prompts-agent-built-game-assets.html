## What already works (read these first, reuse their patterns)

`SmokeTest/` is a passing smoke test:
- `SmokeTest/Scripts/import_glbs.py` imports the three GLBs through Interchange with `AssetImportTask` (headless commandlet). The skinned meshes, skeletons and animation clips all come through with correct clip lengths. Animations land next to the mesh in `.../SkeletalMeshes/`, named `<file><clip>` (for example `scuttlerwalk`).
- `SmokeTest/Source/` is a minimal C++ game module (`AArenaWalker`, `UArenaSmokeLib`) and its Target/Build files.
- `SmokeTest/Scripts/render_smoke.py` builds a level from Python in the full editor and captures PNGs through a `SceneCapture2D` and `RenderingLibrary.export_render_target`.
- `SmokeTest/shots/*.png` are its output. Look at them: they show the problems listed below.

Known pitfalls, already solved, do not rediscover them:
1. Always build through `./ue build`. It sets `UE_TELEMETRY_DIR`; without it UBT 5.8 crashes after linking ("The value cannot be an empty string (Parameter 'path')").
2. A script run through `./ue editor` is executed with `-ExecutePythonScript`. The editor quits as soon as the script returns unless the script calls `unreal.EditorPythonScripting.set_keep_python_script_alive(True)`. Set it back to `False` when your tick callback is done, which lets the editor exit.
3. `export_render_target` writes a PNG with no file extension; rename it to `.png` before reading it.
4. Interchange warns "Node with a skinned mesh is not root. Parent transforms will not affect a skinned mesh." The rifle GLB has a 180 degree yaw on its root node, so expect the rifle and arms to come in rotated; fix orientation in the attachment transform, not by re-exporting.
5. The GLBs are in metres and arrive in UE centimetres. In the smoke test the Blender scuttler is about 254 x 213 x 129 cm and the Tripo scuttler about 174 x 183 x 119 cm. Decide on one sensible gameplay size (a knee-to-waist-high monster, roughly 80 to 120 cm long) and scale both to it.
6. The smoke-test shots are badly lit: models near black, floor blown out, streaky shadows. Causes are a near-black base colour on the Blender scuttler, auto exposure, and shadows captured before they settled. Fix lighting properly in the arena: fixed (manual) exposure, sensible sun and sky intensity, and wait enough frames before capturing.

# Model, rig and animate the boss monster in Blender for an Unreal Engine 5 shooter

You are making the boss for "Scuttler Arena", a small first-person shooter in UE 5.8. The regular enemy is the Scuttler, described in `../creature.txt`; its finished model is `../game/scuttler.glb` (look at `../unreal/Arena/Saved/Shots/scuttler-walk-side.png` and `../arm-video/views/` for how it looks). Work only in this folder. Use Blender in background mode (`blender -b -P script.py`), writing your own bpy scripts.

## The boss: "Brood Mother"

The queen of the Scuttler swarm, same design language so it obviously belongs to the same species: an armoured tick, but about 4 m long and 2.5 m tall. Domed, segmented, cracked shell in charcoal and dark brown, with glowing orange-red sacs or vents along the back (her weak points), a heavy head with large mandibles and the same ring of reddish-brown bristles around the mouth, five thick clawed legs on each side (count them: ten legs), plus two large front scythe-like forelimbs she uses to attack. She should look heavier and more dangerous than the regular Scuttler, not just a scaled copy.

## Requirements

1. Model `brood_mother.blend`: under 25k triangles, at most 3 materials (shell, flesh/legs, emissive weak points). Bake textures (base colour, normal, roughness) at 1024–2048 px from procedural nodes and a high-detail sculpt or displacement if you can, onto a proper UV unwrap. Base colours no darker than about 0.08 linear: earlier assets came out near black in Unreal. Origin at ground centre, facing -Y, metres, standing on z=0.
2. Rig: one armature with body, head, mandibles, a chain per leg, the two forelimbs, and a few shell/abdomen bones for secondary motion. Clean skin weights (no neighbouring legs dragging each other, no shell tearing).
3. Animate, all in place:
   - `idle` (2 s loop): breathing, mandibles twitch, weak points pulse is fine to leave to the engine.
   - `walk` (1.2 s loop): heavy, deliberate gait across all ten legs, body sway.
   - `attack` (1.2 s): rears up and slams both forelimbs down in front of her (the hit frame is about 0.6 s — write the exact time in rounds.md).
   - `roar` (1.5 s): rears up, mandibles open wide, used when she enters.
   - `hit` (0.4 s): flinch.
   - `die` (2 s): collapses heavily, legs curl in, ends still and fully on the ground (nothing under the floor).
4. Export `brood_mother.glb` (glTF binary, all six animations, +Y up). Make sure each animation's timeline starts at 0.
